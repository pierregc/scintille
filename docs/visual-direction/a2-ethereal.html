Lightning · seen from orbit
iAbout this globe

Lightning across the planet, in near real time. Satellites watching the Earth from orbit record the light of each flash they detect, day and night, and every one of them lands here shortly after, on a globe lit by the Sun as it is right now.

What the dots mean

One dot is one lightning flash, detected from orbit by its optical pulse. That includes flashes inside and between clouds, not only those that reach the ground.

Brightness follows the optical energy the satellite measured for that flash. The instruments do not measure exactly the same quantity, so their values are brought to a common scale.

Coverage has gaps. The satellites see only part of the planet, and detection weakens near the edge of their view. Where there is no data, the globe says so: an empty region is not a region without lightning.

Sources

  • NOAA GOES Geostationary Lightning Mapper (GLM), on GOES-East and GOES-West, over the Americas and the surrounding oceans.
  • EUMETSAT MTG Lightning Imager (LI), over Europe, Africa and most of the Atlantic Ocean.

GLM data courtesy of NOAA. Contains modified EUMETSAT data. Earth imagery: NASA Earth Observatory.
